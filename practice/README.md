# Senior System Design Mock Interviews — Scored & Analyzed

> Real system-design **mock interviews** for **senior software engineer** prep — each with the problem, the design produced (with diagrams), the interviewer's **/10 scorecard**, and a gap-by-gap breakdown of **exactly what lost points and how to fix it.**

Every `NN-session/` folder is a **standalone analyzed write-up** ([`README.md`](./01-session/README.md)) backed by the **raw transcript** (`script.md`). This page rolls them up so recurring weak spots turn into a study plan.

📣 **Rehearsal tools:** pin the [Opening Ritual drill card](./opening-ritual.md) — the fixed opening sequence you run before every mock — work through the [Answer Framework playbook](./answer-framework.md) — the 8 steps to cover in every answer — run the [framework's in-the-room checklist](../concepts/00-framework/system-design-interview-framework.md#4-in-the-room-checklist-quick-reference) as a dry-run before each mock — and when re-attempting a scored-below-7 problem, run the [Re-Solve Checklist](./re-solve-checklist.md), the gap-driven drill that forces the two universal misses and the concurrency fix into points. The **crux-solving reflex** is the confirmed #1 weak spot — on write-first problems it's the concurrency guard (it sank S07's counter race), and on **stateful-connection problems it's the connection layer + delivery guarantee** (S08's chat app named both and solved neither fully) — but **S09 is the breakthrough**: re-solving S08's prompt after drilling those gaps produced the **first Pass in the log (✅ 7.5)**, with the connection routing *and* the failover recovery path both volunteered. It's the clearest evidence yet that the re-solve method converts a named crux into a solved one. But **S10 (a brand-new high-frequency trading prompt) drops back to ⚠️ 5.5** — the lowest since S04 — confirming the pattern's other half: a *first* encounter with a hard-crux problem re-exposes the "name-but-don't-solve" ceiling. Its crux, the **order matching engine**, was modeled as a database-backed worker instead of an in-memory single-writer state machine, and the four probed depths (hot partition, real-time price push, 100M-reads caching, the matching algorithm) all stalled — even as genuinely senior instincts (single-writer-per-symbol via consistent hashing, the wallet latency fix, Kafka DLQ fallback) showed up. The two misses that still recur on *every* problem are **capacity estimation** (thin, absent, or decision-free in 9 of 10 — S09's concurrent-connection count was the exception; **S10's numbers were internally inconsistent and drove no decision**) and a **drawn data model** (S09 drew a schema but needed prompting; **S10 put the crux "table" — the order book — in a database when it must live in memory**) (see [How to Improve](#how-to-improve)).

### [03 — E-commerce Platform (Re-attempt of S02)](./03-session/README.md) · ⚠️ 6.5/10

**Same prompt as S02, re-solved after drilling the gaps** — the clearest progress signal so far. **What closed:** the blind spots that failed S02 all appeared *unprompted* — estimation numbers (5k users, 100:1, ~1000 RPS), a **drawn data-model schema**, **Redis caching** + invalidation, a **CDN** for images, and an **auth module** with buyer/seller authZ. ### [04 — Online Auction System (eBay-style)](./04-session/README.md) · ❌ 5.5/10

**First brand-new problem since the S02/S03 re-solves — and the lowest score yet.** Without a rehearsed checklist to lean on, a problem whose *entire point* is concurrency and shared state exposed the two weakest axes head-on: **Problem-Solving and Scale both fell to 5.0**. **What went wrong (three wrong mental models):** thought an auction is **fixed ~5 re-bidding rounds** (it's one hard `end_time`, highest wins); planned to **save all bids and pick the max after the timer** (bid integrity means checking against the current highest *at write time*); wanted to keep the highest bid in an **in-memory max-heap** (lost on crash, diverges across N servers). **The recurring misses returned:** no **Orders** table, no **payment-data / PCI**, a **cluttered diagram** — now 2+ sessions each. **The new gap:** estimation sized daily users but **never the hot auction** (bids/sec on one row, concurrent viewer connections). **What held:** clean service split, reconcile-job backstop, wallet pre-validation, primary-replica failover, and the correct consistency-over-availability call. **Key fixes:** atomic conditional UPDATE / single-writer-per-auction · **Redis ZSET** + append-only bids log · **SSE + pub/sub** to decouple watchers from bid throughput · guarded exactly-once close. → **[Read the full write-up →](./04-session/README.md)**

### [05 — Online Learning Platform (Udemy/Coursera-style)](./05-session/README.md) · ⚠️ 6.5/10

**A rebound after S04 — 5.5 → 6.5 (Lean Hire), and the drilled read-path instincts finally showed up *unprompted*.** **What went well:** **CQRS** (read/write split for courses *and* forum), **event-driven progress tracking** with Kafka (+ correct consumer-durability reasoning), **cache-aside + invalidation**, **CDN for video**, stateless→horizontal scaling, and a crisp **read-your-writes** answer for replica lag — **Scale jumped 5.0 → 7.0**. **What still held it to Lean Hire:** the *same recurring pair* — **no capacity estimation** (S01/S04/S05) and **no data model** (S01/S02/S04/S05, interviewer-flagged again) — plus three domain-specific gaps: **video streaming never explained end-to-end** (no transcoding / HLS-DASH / adaptive bitrate — the heart of the problem), **progress modeled as a lossy Kafka→analytics stream** instead of an idempotent user-facing write, and **search left shallow** (no indexing/CDC). Also needed **prompting** to reach the replication-lag and stale-cache edge cases, and the **CDN was drawn but not wired** to the video path. **Key fixes:** open with storage + egress numbers · draw the `progress` table (upsert on `(student, lesson)`) · transcode → segment (HLS/DASH) → CDN → ABR · authoritative progress write + *derived* analytics · CDC → Elasticsearch. → **[Read the full write-up →](./05-session/README.md)**

### [06 — MVP Social Networking App (startup, cost-constrained)](./06-session/README.md) · ⚠️ 6.5/10

**A constraint-driven prompt — build cheap and ship fast — where the framing was the strength and depth was the gap.** **What went well:** genuine **MVP judgment** — every choice (monolith over microservices, single DB, minimal components, deferred rate limiting) tied back to the cost/ship-speed constraint; a **defended monolith** with clean microservices trade-offs (deployment, network latency, operational cost); a **sequenced scaling path** (vertical → read replica when a hot table saturates → service decomposition) — **Scale held at 7.0**. **What held it to Lean Hire:** the *same universal pair* — **no data model** (interviewer-flagged, now 5 of 6) and **estimation that stopped at user counts** (no read QPS / storage / bandwidth) — plus a **shallow feed** ("all posts, latest first": no pagination, no follow graph, no fan-out decision), **no cache-invalidation story** (asked twice), **authZ left at authN** (can A edit B's post?), and a **diagram that read as a phantom second "read database."** **Key fixes:** draw `users`/`posts`/**`follows`** · use estimation to *justify staying simple* (and name the headroom) · feed = **fan-out-on-read (pull)** now, push later, cursor pagination · **invalidate cache on write + TTL** · ownership authZ + input validation · one labeled `primary` with the cache inline on the read path. → **[Read the full write-up →](./06-session/README.md)**

### [07 — Distributed API Rate Limiter (Stripe / Cloudflare style)](./07-session/README.md) · ⚠️ 6.0/10

**A classic infrastructure problem where the whole design is two hard details — how you count concurrently, and which windowing algorithm you pick — and neither landed.** **What went well:** two genuine **self-corrections** (killed an `Update Tokens Job` doing 10M writes/s in favor of **lazy window reset**; introduced then correctly **dropped consistent hashing** once the shared cache became the source of truth); the **near-accurate (availability-over-strict-consistency) trade-off** reasoned rather than guessed; and a nuanced **fail-open vs fail-closed** call tied to business context. **What held it to Lean-Hire-with-reservations:** the *two crux problems went unsolved* — the **read-modify-write race** on the counter (needs an **atomic `INCR` / Lua script in the store**, never app-side) and the **fixed-window boundary burst** (needs **token bucket / sliding-window counter**, not a smaller window) — plus **estimation that stopped at instance count** (never the ~500 MB counter-memory figure that proves the store is tiny and the DB doesn't belong on the hot path), cache scaling left at "add nodes" with **hot keys unnamed**, **no config/multi-tier limits**, and a **stale diagram** still showing the deleted job. **Problem-Solving fell to 5.0** — on a topic where knowing the standard algorithms is table stakes. **Key fixes:** atomic `INCR`/Lua for the counter · learn the four algorithms cold (fixed window · sliding-window log · sliding-window counter · token/leaky bucket) · counters are ephemeral (Redis + TTL, no durable DB on the hot path) · compute the memory number · keep the diagram live through every pivot. → **[Read the full write-up →](./07-session/README.md)**

### [08 — Real-Time Chat Application (WhatsApp / Slack style)](./08-session/README.md) · ⚠️ 6.5/10

**A rebound from S07 — 6.0 → 6.5, with Design, Problem-Solving, and Communication each ▲1.0 — on a problem whose whole difficulty is the live connection layer.** **What went well:** the clearest *defended iteration* yet — the **sharding key evolved geographic → by-sender → conversation/group ID**, each step motivated by a concrete group-read pattern (this is the senior signal S04's flip-flopping lacked); a genuinely strong **file path** (signed S3 URLs for direct upload, a Kafka **compression pipeline**, **S3→Glacier** cold tiering); **idempotency + at-least-once** named for delivery; and immediate **stateless-gateway horizontal scaling**. **What held it to Borderline:** the *two crux distributed-systems problems went unresolved* — **connection routing** ("more WebSocket servers," but not *how a message finds the server holding the recipient* — needs a **presence registry `user_id → gateway_id` + per-gateway pub/sub**) and **WebSocket failover** (left as "I'm not sure") — plus a **last-mile delivery guarantee** that stopped at "Kafka is at-least-once" instead of **persist-then-ack + a per-recipient sync cursor**; a **muddled SSE-vs-WebSocket** detour before landing on WebSockets; and **read receipts, message ordering, presence, and caching/CDN** left uncovered. **Estimation sized QPS + storage but never the ~1M+ concurrent connections** — the number that actually designs the system. **Key fixes:** presence registry + pub-sub routing · **persist-then-ack + sync cursor** (failover recovery = the reconnect-and-sync path) · per-conversation `seq_no` for ordering + `message_id` dedup · name the fan-out model (push small / pull large) · CDN for 2.5 PB/day egress · size the concurrent-connection count. → **[Read the full write-up →](./08-session/README.md)**

### [09 — Real-Time Chat Application (Re-attempt of S08)](./09-session/README.md) · ✅ 7.5/10

**The first Pass in the log — and proof the re-solve method works.** Re-attempting S08's chat prompt after drilling its two crux gaps flipped **Borderline → Pass (6.5 → 7.5, every axis ▲, Problem-Solving ▲1.5)**. **What closed:** the exact misses that capped S08 appeared *unprompted* — **connection routing** (a **Session Store** `user_id → gateway_id` + a **Delivery Worker** routing to the owning gateway) and **failover recovery** (reconnect + resume from the stored `sequence_id`). The candidate independently reproduced almost the whole S08 **ideal design** — **Kafka backbone + Delivery Worker**, `conversation_id` sharding, per-conversation `sequence_id`, signed-S3-URL + compression pipeline, CDN reads, read-replica + cache, **Redis for ephemeral activity/presence**, SNS/FCM offline push — and **computed the ~1M concurrent-connection number** S08 skipped. Independent saves: file-compression metadata, the User Activity Store design, and a **correct push-back** on cross-shard ordering (same conversation = one shard). **What still held it to a *solid* (not strong) pass — now finer than any prior session:** the **message/file ordering** fix (denormalize into one table with a nullable `file_id`) needed a nudge; **trade-offs were reactive** (consistency-over-availability, batching, caching all arrived when asked); the **delivery guarantee stopped at "Kafka is at-least-once"** without the per-recipient cursor + `message_id` dedup that makes it exact-to-the-device; the **fan-out model** (push small / pull large) went unnamed again; and **failure depth** (connection draining, replication strategy) stayed shallow. **Key fixes:** design the denormalized `messages` table upfront · state persist-then-ack + sync cursor explicitly · **volunteer trade-offs before being asked** (the pass→strong-pass lever) · name the fan-out model · go one level deeper on failure (draining, ×3 cross-AZ, read-your-writes). → **[Read the full write-up →](./09-session/README.md)**

### [10 — High-Frequency Stock Trading System](./10-session/README.md) · ⚠️ 5.5/10

**A brand-new hard-crux prompt after the S09 breakthrough — and a ▼2.0 regression that repeats S04's shape.** Just as S04 fell to the lowest score right after the S02/S03 re-solve, S10 drops to **5.5 (lowest since S04)** on the first fresh problem after S09's Pass — because the drilled habits carry the *periphery* but not the on-the-spot depth on a problem's defining component. **What went well:** genuinely senior instincts landed — **single writer per symbol reached unprompted** ("no two workers on the same stock" → **consistent hashing on `stock_id`**, the concurrency guard the tracker has chased since S01, in its correct partition-for-serialization form); the **wallet optimization** (pulled external payment off the critical trade path when challenged); **Kafka retry → DLQ → wallet-credit / bank fallback** for a failed seller payout; a **read/write consistency split** stated upfront; and a **clean service decomposition** that held Design at 7.0. **What held it to Borderline (Lean No Hire):** the crux — the **order matching engine** — was never designed, modeled as a "Trade Settlement Worker reading DB-backed sorted lists" instead of an **in-memory limit order book per symbol, price-time priority, single-writer thread, durable via a sequenced replicated journal (event sourcing)**; the **hot-stock partition** stalled at "batch processing" (which contradicts ultra-low latency — a single book is *one* serialization point by nature, so you scale *across* symbols, not by batching); **external payment sat in the critical path** until challenged; **real-time price distribution** (100M reads/sec, push vs pull) was never raised; and **estimation was internally inconsistent** (1K vs 100 req/instance, a hand-wave 100K instances) and **drove no decision** — the per-symbol rate (≈200/sec, which dissolves the hot-stock fear), the in-RAM order-book footprint, and the fan-out bandwidth were never derived. **Key fixes:** design the matching engine as the core (in-memory book · price-time priority · single-writer/symbol · journal + replay) · name the concurrency mechanism *as the design*, not under interrogation · reason about latency at every hop (wallet-reserve + async-settle) · reads scale separately from writes — **push** price feeds via WebSocket/SSE + multicast + edge cache · make estimation *decide* something. → **[Read the full write-up →](./10-session/README.md)**

## Consolidated Tips

Grouped by the axis interviewers score, weakest axis first. Session tags like `[S01]` mark the source; a repeat across sessions is a priority to drill. Per-axis score history is shown so trends are visible.

### Problem Solving — `6.5 → 7.0 → 6.0 → 5.0 → 6.0 → 6.0 → 5.0 → 6.0 → 7.5 → 5.0` (⚠️ **S10 ▼2.5 back to 5.0** — a brand-new hard-crux problem re-exposed the ceiling S09 had cleared; the matching engine was named but never designed)
- **Hunt the concurrency edge case — the confirmed #1 recurring miss, now four sessions running.** Missed as an *edge case* in S02/S03 (two buyers grab the last unit); in **S04 it was the *entire problem*** and still never made concrete; in **S07 the counter race was again unsolved** (two instances read the same count, both write count+1 — a malicious client exploits it). Make it reflexive: enforce the invariant *at write time* via an **atomic conditional update** (`UPDATE … WHERE current_price < :bid`, check rows-affected), an **atomic `INCR` / Lua script in the store** (rate-limiter counter), a **row lock**, an **optimistic version check**, or a **single writer per partition**. Atomicity in the store — not app-side read-modify-write, not locks — is the answer. **S10 is the first time the guard was reached *unprompted*** — "no two workers on the same stock" → **consistent hashing on `stock_id`** — the correct partition-for-serialization form; the remaining gap was saying it *as the design* rather than arriving via "first-come-first-serve → one worker → hashing" under questioning. `[S02]` `[S03]` `[S04]` `[S07]` `[S10 ~]` → [concurrency control](../concepts/08-distributed-systems/concurrency-control.md)
- **When you name a hard problem, push one level to the solution — don't stop at "here's the issue."** S07 correctly surfaced *both* crux problems (the read-modify-write race and the fixed-window boundary burst) and solved *neither* — that's exactly where Problem-Solving points went (▼ to 5.0). **S08 repeated it on a new domain:** it named WebSocket connection routing and failover and the last-mile guarantee, then stalled on all three ("I'm not sure how to solve this"). **S09 is the counter-example that proves the drill works** — re-solving S08's prompt after studying the fixes, the connection routing *and* the failover path were both *solved and volunteered*, and Problem-Solving jumped to 7.5. Seeing the problem is half; the drill is forcing "…and here's how I fix it" before moving on — and it converts points. **S10 regressed here on a new problem** — the **order matching engine** (the crux) was named as a "settlement worker" but never designed as an in-memory single-writer state machine, and the **hot-stock partition** was named then abandoned at "batch processing." Same tell as S07/S08: name the defining hazard, stall on the primitive. `[S07]` `[S08]` `[S09 ✓]` `[S10]`
- **Stateful-connection routing = a presence registry + pub/sub, not "more servers."** S08's core miss: with millions of persistent WebSockets, the question is *which gateway holds user X right now* — answered by a **`user_id → gateway_id` registry** (Redis, heartbeat TTL) + **per-gateway pub/sub** so a message routes to the exact owning gateway. Failover is then the *same* path: client auto-reconnects → re-registers → syncs missed messages from its cursor. **S09 landed exactly this unprompted** — a **Session Store** (`user_id → connected_api_gateway_id`) + a **Delivery Worker** routing to the owning gateway, with reconnect-and-resume-from-`sequence_id` as the failover path. On any real-time/chat/presence problem, reach for the registry first. `[S08]` `[S09 ✓]` → [real-time communication](../concepts/04-apis/realtime-communication.md)
- **A delivery guarantee is persist-then-ack + a per-recipient cursor — not "the queue is at-least-once."** S08 stopped at Kafka's pipeline guarantee, which says nothing about the *device*. Persist the message (with a `message_id` + per-conversation `seq_no`) **before** acking the sender; mark delivered only on the client's ack; re-send unacked on reconnect; dedup by `message_id`. Offline delivery, reconnect gap-sync, and gateway failover all collapse to this one cursor-sync path. **S09 got persist-then-ack and reconnect-resume right but *still* stopped at "Kafka is at-least-once"** — the per-recipient `last_delivered_seq` cursor and `message_id` dedup that make it exact-to-the-device stayed implicit. Say the cursor out loud. `[S08]` `[S09]` → [message queue](../concepts/07-messaging-and-events/message-queue.md)
- **On a named-pattern problem, know the standard answers cold — they're table stakes.** A rate limiter *is* atomic-counter + windowing-algorithm; not reaching **token bucket / sliding-window counter** (vs the fixed-window burst) is like designing a URL shortener without base62. Memorize the canonical toolkit for the classic problems. `[S07]` → [concurrency control](../concepts/08-distributed-systems/concurrency-control.md)
- **Keep external I/O off the critical latency path — interrogate every hop.** S10 put an **external payment call (PayPal webhook) in the buy-order path**, directly contradicting the ultra-low-latency requirement, and only pulled it out (a **pre-funded wallet: reserve at order entry, settle async, top-up off-path**) when challenged. On any latency-sensitive design, ask of each step *"does this belong on the hot path?"* — external calls, synchronous DB writes, and locks usually don't. Say the reserve-then-settle pattern proactively. `[S10]` → [non-functional requirements](../concepts/02-foundations/non-functional-requirements.md)
- **Distributed shared state lives in a shared store, never process memory.** S04's in-memory **max-heap** for the highest bid is lost on a crash and diverges across N service instances. Any "track the highest/latest X across servers" → **Redis ZSET + an append-only durable log** to rebuild from. `[S04]` → [Redis ZSET](../concepts/06-caching/redis-sorted-sets.md)
- **Offer 2–3 options before committing, then commit.** S04 *flip-flopped* — lock → save-all-and-pick → SSE-vs-polling — and landed decisions on none. Name the options and their trade-offs, then pick one and defend it. `[S01]` `[S04]`
- **Model the domain before the mechanism.** S04's "fixed ~5 rounds" detour came from not knowing how an auction actually *ends* — one `end_time`, highest bid wins. Understand termination/state before designing the machinery. `[S04]`

12. **Surface edge cases proactively + keep the diagram legible, live, *and* fully wired.** Volunteer replication-lag/stale-cache risks before being asked (S05/S06 needed prompting; **S08's read receipts, ordering, and presence all went uncovered until flagged**). Diagram arc: *stale* (S02) → *cluttered* (S03, S04) → **unconnected CDN (S05)** → **phantom "read database" (S06)** → **never updated after the redesign (S07)** → **legible and well-wired at last (S08 ✓, lifted Communication to 7.0)** — the fix landed once, now make it the default: **redraw on every pivot**, separate read vs write (and allow/deny/check) flows, color per journey, **wire every box you draw**.
13. **Don't stop at bottleneck #1.** After read replicas + cache, name the next ones: **horizontal backend scale** behind the LB, **DB sharding**, **rate limiting** at the gateway, connection pooling.
14. **Study the recurring fundamentals** that cut across problems: **consistent hashing, base62 encoding, HTTP 3xx redirects, cache eviction (LRU/LFU/TTL), sharding/partitioning, load balancing, Redis ZSET.**
15. **Keep external I/O off the critical latency path.** S10 placed an **external payment call in the buy-order path** on an ultra-low-latency system, fixing it (**pre-funded wallet: reserve at entry, settle async, top-up off-path**) only when challenged. On any latency-sensitive design, interrogate every hop — *"does this belong on the hot path?"* — and move external calls, synchronous DB writes, and locks off it. Volunteer the reserve-then-settle / journal-then-project pattern. → [non-functional requirements](../concepts/02-foundations/non-functional-requirements.md)

## How to Improve

### The diagnosis

**S03 proved a habit fix works; S04 proved it isn't enough; S05–S08 mapped the ceiling; S09 broke through it via re-solve — and S10 confirmed the ceiling is still there on a *fresh* hard problem.** Re-solving S02's problem flipped **FAIL → Lean Hire** as the read-path habits (schema, caching, CDN, auth) appeared unprompted. **S04** — a brand-new *write-first* problem — fell to the **lowest yet (5.5)** because those habits do nothing for on-the-spot write-path reasoning. **S05** — a read-heavy problem — **rebounded to 6.5** as the read-path scaling stack fired unprompted. **S06** — an MVP/cost-constrained problem — **held at 6.5** on strong constraint framing. **S07** — a distributed rate limiter — **dipped to 6.0**: a problem whose *entire difficulty is a concurrency primitive* exposed the weakest axis (Problem-Solving 5.0) because the crux was *named but unsolved*. **S08** — a real-time chat app — **rebounded to 6.5**, generalizing the lesson: the "name-but-don't-solve" ceiling recurred on **stateful-connection routing and the delivery guarantee** (both named, neither fully solved). **S09 — the re-solve of S08 — is the breakthrough: the first clear Pass (✅ 7.5), every axis ▲, Problem-Solving ▲1.5.** After drilling S08's two crux gaps, the candidate *solved and volunteered* both (Session Store + Delivery Worker routing; reconnect-resume failover), reproduced almost the entire S08 ideal design unprompted, and computed the ~1M concurrent-connection number. **S10 — a brand-new high-frequency-trading prompt — then fell to ⚠️ 5.5 (lowest since S04), repeating S04's exact shape: a strong re-solve, then a first hard-crux problem that re-exposes the ceiling.** The crux, the **order matching engine**, was modeled as a DB-backed settlement worker rather than an in-memory single-writer state machine; the hot-partition, real-time price push, and 100M-reads caching all stalled — even as real senior instincts appeared (single-writer-per-symbol *unprompted*, the wallet latency fix, Kafka DLQ fallback, an upfront consistency split). So the pattern is now proven in every direction: **the drilled habits carry read-heavy and constraint-framed problems to Lean Hire, and reliably transfer the *periphery* (decomposition, event-driven tail, consistency split — Design held at 7.0 even here); the "name-but-don't-solve" ceiling is real on a *first* encounter with a hard crux (S04 5.5, S07 6.0, S08 6.5, S10 5.5) — but a targeted re-solve converts the named crux into a solved one and clears the pass bar (S08 6.5 → S09 7.5).** The lever is unchanged: **go deep on the one defining component under your own steam** — and the proven remedy for a sub-7 problem is to re-solve it after drilling exactly that crux. The next tier beyond the pass bar (Pass → Strong Pass, S09's remaining gap): *proactive* trade-offs, an upfront-correct crux schema, and one level more failure-handling depth.

What S05–S10 confirmed (the recurring, problem-independent gaps):

1. **Capacity estimation is thin, absent, or decision-free on nearly every session (9 of 10).** S05 produced *no numbers*; **S06 sized users/posts but not reads/storage/bandwidth**; **S07 derived QPS + instance count but never the ~500 MB counter-memory figure**; **S08 sized message QPS + 2.5 PB/day storage but never the ~1M+ concurrent connections** that demand a stateful connection tier; **S10's numbers were internally inconsistent (1K vs 100 req/instance, hand-wave 100K instances) and drove no decision** (never the per-symbol rate, order-book memory, or fan-out bandwidth). Only S09 broke through. The pattern isn't "no numbers" so much as "stops before the number that *forces a decision*, or gets there inconsistently." This is the single most consistent hole.
2. **The data model is missed or partial 8 of 10 sessions** — interviewer-flagged *again* in S05 and S06. S05's crux was `progress`; S06's was `follows`; S07's was the ephemeral Redis counter + rules table; **S08's was messages sharded by `conversation_id` + a sync cursor** — none drawn (S08 drew architecture but no schema); **S10 drew the datastores but put the crux in the wrong medium** — an order book in a database matched by a worker, when it must be an **in-memory single-writer book + a replicated journal**. The crux artifact — and *where it lives* — is what anchors the design, and it keeps going undrawn or mis-placed.
3. **Per-domain depth is the Lean-Hire→Hire lever — and on named-pattern problems it's table stakes.** S05 named the video pipeline but couldn't explain it; S06's feed had no fan-out decision; S07 never reached token-bucket / atomic `INCR`; **S08 named WebSocket connections + delivery guarantees but couldn't route to a connection, handle failover, or guarantee the last mile**; **S10 named the trade-matching problem but never designed the order matching engine** — in-memory limit order book, price-time priority, single-writer thread, journal + replay are what a trading system *is*. On a canonical problem, not knowing the standard toolkit reads as a knowledge gap, not just shallow depth.
4. **Proactivity still costs points — though delivery is turning.** Edge-case answers came only *when asked* (S05/S06; **S08's read receipts, ordering, and presence all surfaced only when flagged**; **S10's wallet fix and hot-partition only under interviewer pressure**). The diagram **landed legibly in S08–S09** (→ Communication 7.0 → 8.0) after phantom-ambiguous (S06) and stale (S07) — but **S10's verbal walkthrough regressed to verbose/unfocused (Comm 6.0)**, showing structure is the first thing to slip on an unfamiliar problem.

What S04, S07, S08, and now S10 exposed — still open on hard-crux problems:

1. **The crux-solving reflex is the confirmed #1 gap — spanning concurrency, stateful connections, *and* now stateful in-memory processing.** An *edge case* in S02/S03, the *entire problem* in S04, the unsolved counter race in S07, the connection-routing/failover/last-mile misses in **S08**, and in **S10 the order matching engine — the heart of the system — named but never designed** (modeled as a DB-backed worker). The tell is identical across all of them: **I can *name* the defining hazard but can't reach the primitive that fixes it** — atomic conditional update / `INCR`/Lua / single-writer for write-path; **presence registry + pub/sub + persist-then-ack cursor for connection-path**; **in-memory single-writer book + sequenced journal for matching**. Encouragingly, S10 *did* reach single-writer-per-symbol unprompted — the primitive was right, the depth on the engine's core loop was not. It's the instinct to interrogate *and resolve* the crux, and it's the single biggest scoring lever.
2. **"Name the problem, then push to the solution" is the missing half-step.** S07, S08, and S10 all correctly spotted their crux issues and stopped there (S10's hot partition ended at "batch processing," which the interviewer noted contradicts the latency requirement). Recognizing the hazard is necessary but only scores when paired with the concrete fix — this is now the clearest cross-session pattern.
3. **Latency-critical-path discipline is a new named gap.** S10 placed an **external payment call in the critical trade path** on an ultra-low-latency system — a fundamental error, fixed only when challenged. Interrogate every hop for whether it belongs on the hot path; keep external I/O, synchronous writes, and locks off it (reserve-then-settle, journal-then-project).
4. **The write/checkout-path silent boxes remain open** — a **proper Orders model** and **payment-data / PCI** (tokenize, idempotency key) from S03/S04, plus **ephemeral vs durable state** (S07). **S08 adds the read path** (caching hot group membership, a **CDN for 2.5 PB/day of media egress**); **S10 adds the read/write path split at extreme scale** — a **100M-reads/sec price feed** must be a **push/fan-out** tier, decoupled from the match path.

### The plan

- **Make estimation and the schema non-negotiable opening moves** — the two misses that recur on *every* problem (estimation 9/10, schema 8/10). Never start the design without a capacity block (DAU, read:write, QPS, **storage + bandwidth**), and always push to **the one number that forces the key decision** (S07's ~500 MB counter memory; a media platform's egress Gbps; **S10's ~200 trades/sec-per-symbol that dissolves the hot-stock fear, and the 100M reads/sec that forces a push tier**). Keep the numbers *internally consistent* — S10's contradictory per-instance figures scored nothing. Never finish without drawing the crux table/store **in the right medium** (S10's order book is in-memory, not a DB row). On constraint-framed problems, use estimation *in reverse* — let the numbers *justify* the simple design. These two alone are the difference between Lean Hire and Hire.
- **Learn the canonical toolkit for the named problems cold** — rate limiter → **atomic counter (`INCR`/Lua) + token-bucket / sliding-window** (know all four algorithms); media → transcode → HLS/DASH → CDN → ABR; search → CDC → Elasticsearch; social feed → fan-out (pull vs push) + follow graph + cursor pagination; **chat/real-time → WebSocket gateways + presence registry (`user_id → gateway_id`) + per-gateway pub/sub + persist-then-ack sync cursor + APNs/FCM for offline**; **trading/matching → in-memory limit order book per symbol + price-time priority + single-writer thread + sequenced journal & deterministic replay + separate push feed for prices**; auction → concurrency guard. On a classic problem the interviewer expects the standard answer — depth here is table stakes, not bonus.
- **Drill the concurrency primitive as a reflex — and push past naming it to solving it.** On every stock / bid / booking / counter / rate-limit problem, force "two clients race for the same thing" *and answer it cold*: atomic conditional update / **atomic `INCR` or Lua in the store** / optimistic version / **single-writer-per-partition** (S10 reached this unprompted — the primitive was right; the miss was not designing the book's core match loop on top of it). S04 and S07 *spotted* the race and *stalled* on the fix — the score lives in the fix, and in the depth beneath it.
- **Keep external I/O, synchronous writes, and locks off the latency-critical path.** S10 put an external payment call *in* the trade path on an ultra-low-latency system. Interrogate every hop: reserve-then-settle (funds reserved at entry, cash moved async), journal-then-project (durable log on the hot path, DB writes downstream), and push-don't-poll for reads. If a hop can fail slowly, it doesn't belong on the critical path.
- **Distinguish ephemeral from durable state** — before putting a DB on a per-request path, ask "does this need durability or just speed?" Counters, sessions, and rate-limit state are ephemeral (Redis + TTL); a durable DB there only invents a cache-miss-flood problem. Keep the DB for rules/config.
- **Add the write-path items to the opening checklist** — after the data model, ask **"where's the concurrent write, and how do I guard it?"**, **"is there sensitive data (payments/PII) — how is it secured?"**, and **"what's the hot key, and what's its peak load?"**
- **Redraw the diagram on every pivot** — six sessions of diagram notes (stale → cluttered → unconnected → phantom → stale-again). Separate read/write/allow/deny flows, label `primary`, wire every box, and *update it the moment the design changes*.
- **Frame scale as the hot key first** — before aggregate QPS, size the hottest single unit of load (the hot auction, the hot client) and its mitigation (pub/sub, local pre-aggregation, dedicated shard).
- **Keep re-solving — it's now proven twice, and S10 shows why it's needed.** The same-problem re-attempt is the clearest progress signal: S02→S03 flipped FAIL→Lean Hire, and **S08→S09 flipped Borderline→Pass** by targeting exactly the named-but-unsolved crux (presence registry + routing + failover recovery). **S10 confirms the flip side** — a *brand-new* hard-crux problem (trade matching) re-exposed the ceiling (⚠️ 5.5), exactly as S04 did after S03. The remedy is the same: re-solve it after drilling the crux. The next natural re-solves: **S10** (trading) targeting *the in-memory matching engine core loop + journal/replay + a push read tier*; **S07** (rate limiter) targeting *atomic `INCR`/Lua + token-bucket + a live diagram*; or **S04** (auction) targeting *concrete concurrency handling*. And a **second re-solve of the chat prompt** could push S09's 7.5 → 8+ by *volunteering* the trade-offs (consistency split, fan-out model) and stating the delivery cursor explicitly.

> **Implication**: the drilled habits reliably carry read-heavy (S05), constraint-framed (S06), and well-structured stateful problems toward Lean Hire — and **a targeted re-solve now demonstrably clears the pass bar (S08 6.5 → S09 ✅ 7.5, every axis ▲)** by converting the named-but-unsolved crux into a solved, volunteered one. But **S10 (⚠️ 5.5, a fresh trade-matching problem) proves the "name-but-don't-solve" ceiling is still there on a *first* hard-crux encounter** — the same shape as S04 after S03. The pattern is now unmistakable: *re-solves clear the bar; first contact with a hard crux re-exposes it.* The two levers are therefore (1) **re-solve the sub-7 problems** after drilling their exact crux, and (2) **on any new problem, go one level deeper on the defining primitive** rather than naming it and moving on — S10 named the matching engine, single-writer, and Kafka DLQ correctly but never designed the engine's core loop. **Beyond the pass bar (Pass → Strong Pass 8+): (a) the two universal misses are closing but fragile — estimation reached the deciding number in S09 yet went inconsistent and decision-free again in S10, and the schema now gets *drawn* but must be in the *right medium* (in-memory book, not a DB row); (b) *proactivity* — volunteer trade-offs and edge cases rather than waiting to be asked (S09's and S10's fixes were both reactive); (c) keep external I/O off the latency path; (d) one level more depth on the defining primitive and its failure handling.** Split practice ~25% estimation + upfront crux-schema in the right medium, ~35% proactive trade-off/edge-case surfacing (the Pass→Strong-Pass lever), ~25% depth on the defining primitive and hot-path discipline (matching loop, delivery cursor, fan-out model, failure modes), ~15% per-domain toolkit breadth for the next brand-new problem.
